03 / 2026 outlook

Approximately 331,300 jobs.

An early-year extrapolation based on local employment.

Average monthly employment across Region 2 was 329,004 in January–March 2026, compared with 327,528 in January–March 2025. That is a year-over-year gain of 1,476 jobs, or 0.4506%.

Projection: 329,821 × (329,004 ÷ 327,528) = 331,307.3 average jobs. Rounded: approximately 331,300, or about 1,500 more jobs than in 2025.

This assumes the first quarter’s year-over-year growth rate holds across the full year. It is our extrapolation, not an official BLS forecast, and does not incorporate later hiring, layoffs, or revisions. Quarterly employment is not seasonally adjusted; the comparison uses the same three months in both years.

The employment projection and projected net gain are rounded separately to the nearest 100. Their displayed difference may vary slightly because of rounding.

04 / Industry analysis

Where employment is expanding—and where it is contracting.

Region 2’s employment gains have been concentrated in a few parts of the economy. The overall increase conceals substantial differences between industries.

The latest complete annual data show a region still adding jobs, but with limited private-sector expansion in 2025. State government employment increased by 409 jobs and local government employment by 292, while federal employment declined by 11. Together, those government categories added approximately 690 jobs, compared with a net private-sector increase of just 43. Government therefore supplied most of the region’s 2025 net gain. Small differences between these components and the overall regional total reflect independently rounded annual averages.

Education and health services anchor longer-term growth

Private education and health services recorded an annual average of 57,038 jobs in 2025, up 351 from 2024 and 4,276 from 2022. The three-year increase of approximately 8.1% exceeded the entire private sector’s net increase of 2,810 jobs. Expansion in this group was partly offset by contractions elsewhere. These figures cover private employers; public schools and universities are included in government employment.

Leisure and hospitality also contributed to the longer-term increase, adding 1,569 jobs between 2022 and 2025. Its 2025 gain was a more modest 220 jobs. Both groups have published comparable figures across all 18 Region 2 localities.

A rebound in trade and transportation, softer business services

Private trade, transportation, and utilities added 558 jobs in 2025, bringing annual average employment to 58,959. Despite that rebound, employment remained 62 jobs below its 2022 level. Retail trade alone added just 25 jobs in 2025; most of the broader group’s increase came from other components.

Professional and business services moved in the opposite direction. Employment fell by 137 jobs in 2025 to 35,316, leaving the group 1,277 jobs below 2022. The category encompasses professional firms, company management, staffing, and administrative services. Its decline does not establish a corresponding decline in technology occupations: industry classifications describe employers, while technology workers can be employed across many industries.

Construction and manufacturing offer additional, incomplete signals

Among eight localities with disclosed comparable construction figures, employment increased by 635 jobs, or 4.8%, in 2025. Among 14 localities with disclosed comparable manufacturing figures, employment declined by 1,116 jobs, or 2.9%. Those are changes within the published subsets, not complete regional totals. Because the geographic coverage differs, they should not be treated as a ranking of the region’s largest industry gains and losses.

BLS withholds some county-industry records to protect employer confidentiality. Suppressed figures are excluded from these comparisons rather than counted as zero. The broader education and health, leisure and hospitality, trade and transportation, and professional and business services comparisons above each cover all 18 localities.

Employment growth and hiring answer different questions

These patterns identify where the stock of filled jobs is expanding or contracting. They do not measure gross hiring, replacement demand, or current job openings. Employers can recruit actively even when their industry’s total employment is flat or declining. Understanding the occupations being recruited would require a separate occupational or job-postings analysis.

05 / Data & attribution

Public data.
Independent analysis.

We analyze large public administrative datasets to identify regional trends. The underlying employment data are collected and published by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics in cooperation with state workforce agencies.

Attribution: These are BLS data, not data collected or owned by Chip Bobbert. This page presents independent aggregation, analysis, visualization, and a clearly labeled projection. BLS does not sponsor or endorse this analysis.

What is counted: Filled covered jobs at establishments, whether full-time or part-time, temporary or permanent. Private and government employment are included. A person with two covered jobs can be counted twice. Self-employment, unpaid family work, and some other categories are excluded. These counts are not full-time-equivalent positions, vacancies, gross hires, or unique workers.

Method: Sum county-equivalent rows for all industries (industry 10), all ownerships (ownership 0), and all establishment sizes (size 0). Annual employment is the average of monthly job counts. For this initial analysis, 2023 and 2025 use published annual levels; 2022 and 2024 are recovered from each following year’s annual level minus its published exact over-the-year employment change. The first-quarter 2025 comparison is recovered in the same way from the 2026 monthly levels and exact prior-year changes. No suppressed records are treated as zero. Historical values may be revised.

Regional coverage: Counties of Alleghany, Amherst, Appomattox, Bedford, Botetourt, Campbell, Craig, Floyd, Franklin, Giles, Montgomery, Pulaski, and Roanoke; independent cities of Covington, Lynchburg, Radford, Roanoke, and Salem. Counties and cities are counted separately.

Refresh: Checked monthly on the 16th. QCEW local employment releases are quarterly; a monthly check does not imply a new monthly BLS release. Reported totals, source periods, and projections change only after new or revised source data are validated. Annual history and current-year projections are kept separate.
